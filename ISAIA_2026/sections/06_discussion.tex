\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Implications for AI System Evaluation}

Because four of the five mismatch dimensions ($\delta_S$, $\delta_K$,
$\delta_C$, $\delta_T$) are computable from the agent's declared profile
and the task requirements alone, ARS can serve as a pre-execution
deployment gate: agents scoring below $\theta^*$ are escalated to human
analysts rather than executing autonomously. This is consistent with the
NIST AI Risk Management Framework~\cite{nist_ai_rmf}, which recommends
continuous monitoring against operational requirements.

The task-class-specific weight vectors (Table~\ref{tab:weights_isaia})
reveal which dimensions matter most per threat type: ransomware detection
prioritises knowledge coverage ($w_K = 0.35$), while DDoS mitigation is
dominated by skill and capability access ($w_S + w_C = 0.60$). This
enables targeted agent improvement rather than generic capability
enhancement.

\subsection{Trustworthy Deployment of AI Agents}

The Epistemic Gap supports runtime monitoring: comparing stated confidence
against known knowledge coverage flags high-risk outputs for human review
before they trigger automated actions. The temporal-drift model
($\delta_T$) gives a quantitative basis for scheduling knowledge
updates---for high-volatility domains like ransomware ($\lambda = 0.005$),
ARS degrades below $\theta^*$ in roughly $120$ days, suggesting a quarterly
refresh. The cascade bound (Theorem~\ref{thm:cascade}) implies that
tightly-coupled pipeline decomposition cannot improve reliability beyond
the weakest agent, so single-agent designs should be preferred for critical
tasks unless per-agent reliability is independently verified.

\subsection{Limitations}

\textbf{LLM specificity.} The primary experiments use Mistral-7B-Instruct;
absolute thresholds will require recalibration for larger models, though
directional findings should hold since the SKC model is
architecture-agnostic. The cross-model-family results in
Section~\ref{sec:experimental} provide initial evidence for this: under a
forced-answer evaluation, the knowledge--hallucination relationship
replicates across GPT-4o-mini, Claude~Haiku~4.5, and Claude~Sonnet~4.6
(bucket-aggregated $\rho \in [0.78, 0.95]$), indicating that the
\textit{shape} of the signal transfers even though the \textit{threshold}
must be re-fit per backbone. A systematic multi-backbone
recalibration study, spanning open- and closed-weight models at several
parameter scales and reporting per-model $\theta^*$, is a natural next
step.
\textbf{Linear formulation.} The additive ARS is chosen for
interpretability and decomposability; nonlinear alternatives may improve
predictive power at the cost of transparency.
\textbf{Synthetic data.} The dataset is generated from ATT\&CK and NVD;
real-world SOC incidents introduce additional noise and ambiguity.
\textbf{Cascade model.} The formulation assumes acyclic pipelines and
independent mismatch factors; cyclic architectures and correlated
dimensions require further modelling.
\textbf{Controlled benchmark.} Because the benchmark uses controlled
mismatch injection, real SOC deployments may exhibit noisier and
less separable failure patterns.
